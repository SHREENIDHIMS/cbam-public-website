Carbon price relief exists to avoid charging for the same emissions twice. If the emissions in your imported goods have already been subject to a carbon price in the country of production, the UK charge can be reduced by that amount, provided you can prove it.

Relief is a claim, not an automatic deduction. It depends on the scheme, on the evidence and on the way the amount is worked out. This guide describes the idea and the evidence. It does not tell you whether any of your goods qualify.

## Where relief fits in the calculation

The policy summary expresses the CBAM calculation as embodied emissions multiplied by the CBAM rate, less carbon price relief. Relief therefore comes off the end of the calculation. The [guide to what UK CBAM is](/guides/what-is-uk-cbam) places it alongside the other inputs.

The 2026 regulations describe the relief for a good as its effective carbon price multiplied by the emissions embodied in that good, disregarding any emissions that were not subject to a qualifying scheme. The effective carbon price is worked out in steps: identify the relevant emissions, find which of them the scheme covered, multiply by the applicable prices, divide by the total emissions to get a price per tonne, and then adjust for any monetary support the installation received.

## What counts as a qualifying scheme

HMRC says a scheme qualifies only if it meets all of the following:

- it is run by, or on behalf of, a government or a supra-national organisation;
- that body controls how the revenue is used;
- installations that make or process CBAM goods are required by law to take part;
- the rules and scope are publicly available;
- it either prices carbon dioxide directly per tonne, or prices it indirectly through fossil fuel use, using emissions factors from recognised bodies.

Schemes that are voluntary, run by non-governmental organisations, based on fuel duties, or that do not charge for production emissions do not qualify. HMRC has published a provisional list of current qualifying schemes to help importers prepare. Because the list is provisional, check it again before you rely on it.

## The three conditions for a claim

To claim relief, HMRC's guidance says three conditions must be met:

1. the embodied emissions were subject to a qualifying carbon pricing scheme;
2. you obtained a completed carbon pricing verification form;
3. the form was completed by an independent verifier who meets the necessary standards.

HMRC lays out the process in four steps: check whether your goods were subject to a qualifying scheme, get a carbon pricing verification form, gather what you need to work out relief, and then work out the relief.

## Why evidence matters

The verification form is the centre of a claim. HMRC's record-keeping guidance says to keep the carbon pricing verification forms, the details of the qualifying scheme, the verified effective carbon price calculations and the exchange rates you used to convert amounts into sterling. These need to be kept for <Rule k="recordRetention" />, counted from the later of the date the record was created and the end of the accounting period.

A producer's statement that a carbon price was paid is a starting point, not proof. If it is not backed by a verification form from a suitable verifier, you should not assume relief will be available.

## Evidence checklist

- The producer and installation that made the goods.
- The scheme the installation takes part in, checked against the current list.
- A completed carbon pricing verification form for each good you claim for.
- Confirmation of who verified it and that they meet the requirements.
- The effective carbon price calculation, with its inputs.
- The exchange rates used to convert into sterling.
- Any monetary support the installation received, recorded as the form requires.
